Four Lenses for the Tiger Team

Same news, four different morning meetings.

IT · Infra & Security

An AI-generated 2FA bypass was caught mid-flight this week — the first confirmed AI-built zero-day intended for mass exploitation. Meanwhile, 48% of zero-days exploited in 2025 hit enterprise infrastructure (VPNs, firewalls, identity, virtualization), and AI-native vulns inside copilots and agents are the new frontier. Anthropic’s Project Glasswing is quietly arming AWS, Apple, Cisco, Google, JPM, and Microsoft with Mythos to hunt zero-days first. (Google Cloud)

So what for Scantron? Treat any AI copilot or agent with access to test content, student PII, or scoring pipelines as a high-value attack surface. Stand up an AI-asset inventory and a 2FA-bypass tabletop this quarter — before the next headline names a testing vendor.

People & HR · Workforce

OpenAI is nearly doubling headcount (4,500 → 8,000), Pluralsight’s AI Academy scaled from 500 to 100,000+ participants, and the U.S. Department of Labor launched a national push to weave AI skills into Registered Apprenticeships. Demand is concentrated in MLOps, forward-deployed engineers, AI governance, and — quietly — data annotation. (Computerworld, U.S. DOL)

So what for Scantron? Stop hiring AI engineers in isolation. Build a tiered AI literacy track (foundations → tool fluency → agent-builders) and tie it to certification — we already speak that language. Also: an AI governance lead is no longer a nice-to-have.

Ops & Finance · Process & Risk

87% of North American CFOs now call AI “extremely or very important” to finance ops for 2026; teams using it save ~21 hours/week, and Gartner expects embedded AI in cloud ERPs to drive a 30% faster financial close by 2028. Reconciliations, variance analysis, intercompany, and compliance checks are the cleanest agent-target zones today. (Journal of Accountancy, Gartner)

So what for Scantron? Pick one rule-heavy, measurable workflow (AP, scan-to-score reconciliation, vendor onboarding) and put a bounded agent on it this quarter. Measure cycle time and error rate, not vibes.

Marketing & Sales · GTM & CX

Agentic AI is the headline trend across CX in 2026, but only 41% of marketers can prove AI ROI — not because value is missing, but because boards now demand it in business outcomes, not impressions. Klarna’s genAI assistant handled the workload of 700 full-time agents in month one; about half of customers still want a human escape hatch. (eMarketer, Jasper State of AI Marketing)

So what for Scantron? Reposition the brand from “the bubble sheet people” to “the trusted-assessment people in an AI world.” Lead with integrity, auditability, and data lineage — the boring superpowers districts can’t buy from a chatbot.

This week we lean into the right column — Opportunities and Threats — because that’s where this week’s news lands.

Strengths

Weaknesses

  2. Ship an AI Coach analog tied to scan-derived data — following NWEA’s Reading Fluency Coach pattern, but across our broader assessment footprint.

  5. Bounded agents for ops (Gartner: 30% faster close by 2028) — ROI inside our own four walls before we sell it.

  2. Frontier model commoditization of scoring. Opus 4.7 and GPT-5.5 make “good-enough” AI scoring accessible to scrappier entrants — the moat shrinks unless we own provenance.

  4. NWEA & Pearson are already “the AI assessment story.” Mindshare gap compounds quarter over quarter.
  5. Supply chain & AI-native vulns. Recent open-source supply-chain compromises (Trivy, LiteLLM, Checkmarx) raise the bar on what we accept from vendors and OSS dependencies.

Gen AI 101 — What is an “Agent”?

Everyone says “AI agent” this week. Here’s the plain-English version, in three sentences.

A chatbot answers a question. An agent takes a goal, breaks it into steps, calls tools (search the web, query a database, send an email, edit a file), checks its own work, and tries again if it’s wrong — until the goal is done or it gives up. Think “intern with a to-do list and access to your software,” not “search box with manners.”

Why it matters now: this week’s ServiceNow, Google, Mistral, and Microsoft launches all promise agents that can act inside your systems. Whether you trust them is mostly a question of permissions and guardrails, not intelligence.

Inspiration — the Reading Coach in the Room

NWEA quietly shipped Coach inside its MAP Reading Fluency assessment — an AI tutor that listens to a child read, then offers personalized practice based on what it heard. No leaderboard. No badges. No dystopia. Just “you stumbled on multi-syllable words today, let’s try a few more.”

That is the version of AI we should be building toward: invisible, patient, in service of a single kid getting better at one thing. It’s a useful north star when the cycle gets loud. (NWEA)

Grounding — A Reality Check

Microsoft expects to spend $190 billion on capex in 2026 against $37 billion of AI ARR. That’s not a typo; that’s the math. Hyperscalers are betting that demand catches up with supply before Wall Street loses patience. (Crescendo)

For us — and most enterprise buyers — that means two things. One, model prices and capabilities will keep moving; don’t lock multi-year contracts on today’s frontier. Two, if your AI ROI story relies on the model getting cheaper, it probably will — but the integration, change management, and trust work will not. Plan for the boring part to dominate the bill.
